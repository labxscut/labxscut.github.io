When the source slug resolves to a publication or abstract record, the card gains a venue line and chips that link the lab authors (to their /team/ pages), the tools, and the record on /publications/. Links are derived only from data already in the registry, never guessed. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign d = item.date_display | default: item.date | append: "" -%} {%- assign y = d | slice: 0, 4 -%} {%- assign mo = d | slice: 4, 2 -%} {%- assign dy = d | slice: 6, 2 -%} {%- assign slug = "" -%} {%- if item.source contains "/Paper/" -%} {%- assign slug = item.source | split: "/Paper/" | last -%} {%- endif -%} {%- assign paper = site.data.publications | where: "slug", slug | first -%} {%- unless paper -%} {%- assign paper = site.data.abstracts | where: "slug", slug | first -%} {%- endunless -%} {%- comment -%} The news aggregator records the registry folder it announced, but gen_publications.py collapses Feishu/git twin records and keeps the survivor's slug, so that folder name is often not the slug the site publishes. Resolve it two ways, both derived only from registry data (never guessed): 1. `alias_slugs` — the exact list of twin slugs the generator collapsed into each published record (the authoritative mapping); 2. the entry's own DOI, which is unique across the site data. {%- endcomment -%} {%- unless paper -%} {%- comment -%} Plain loop rather than `where_exp … contains`: the runtime is Liquid 4.0 and most records have no `alias_slugs` at all, so `nil contains x` would have to be trusted.
